# ANIMATE — Commercial and product evidence gate

**Status: proposed research protocol only. No customer outreach, paid pilot, purchase, acquisition offer, iPhone field study or demand result has occurred.** This document is not a market forecast or a claim of competitive superiority.

## What the current engineering results do — and do not — establish

| Claim | Evidence currently available | Required before making the claim |
|---|---|---|
| Local hand landmark inference can execute | Pinned test-image inference succeeded in Chromium and Linux WebKit CI | Approved model redistribution rights, real-device inference and repeatable trials |
| Secure handling of browser research data | Automated source, browser and hosted-preview adversarial tests | Approved host, operator privacy controls and live access audit |
| Consistent performance on iPhone | No physical-device observations; an operator measurement tool is prepared | Separately approved HTTPS preview and multiple real iPhone trials with frame-rate coverage, latency, error rates, battery and heat observations |
| Accurate interaction in realistic conditions | Synthetic scenarios and manually labelled camera-proxy benchmarks only | Independent ground-truth success/failure counts with false-positive/false-negative denominators and adverse lighting/backgrounds |
| Consumers demand the product | No evidence. A demonstration and voluntary feedback are not purchase behaviour | Prospective users drawn independently from one specified segment, with the real alternative and switching cost documented |
| Acquirers would buy this software | No verified acquirer inquiries, offers or due diligence completed | Independently qualified buyer conversations and documented acquisition interest, terms and barriers |

## Distinct evidence tracks

**Technical usability (not demand):** An operator tests one exact build on a physical iPhone using the [device protocol](IPHONE_DEVICE_METRICS_PROTOCOL.md). A complete 60-second sample requires adequate continuous throughput. This is not a classification accuracy certificate; record ground-truth challenge attempts, false zone completions and failures separately. Do not pool hand-tracking, colour-tracking and foreground-tracking outcomes.

**User problem and adoption (not acquisition intent):** Choose one specific first-use context *before* interviewing anyone (for example, a precise teacher classroom activity rather than generic 'education'). Interview independent people who already perform that task. Record their current workaround, frequency, failure cost, constraints and whether a realistic camera-controlled alternative would replace it. Separate requests for novelty demonstrations from recurring use.

**Commercial willingness to pay:** Discuss a concrete deliverable and a real stated price with authorised buyers, not a free demo alone. A response of 'interesting', a hypothetical survey answer or positive sentiment is not a sale. Keep demonstrable commitments (paid order, signed conditional pilot or comparable independently verifiable commitment) separately from expressions of curiosity. No payment collection or external outreach is authorised merely by this document.

**Build-to-sell acquisition evidence:** The purchaser of the source code may be different from the end user. Verify whether a qualified software buyer values the intellectual property, licensing chain, repeatable deployment process, customer access, maintenance cost and evidence of demand. Distinguish an actual signed acquisition offer from an unactionable valuation estimate. Do not claim an expected exit price without external market evidence.

## Pre-registration before any permitted field trial

1. Name one primary segment, use case and measurable competing alternative, and independently record why that segment matters. Do not assume that a self-selected UI role proves market size.
2. Choose a sample size, recruitment channel, exposure period, acceptance criteria and stop conditions **before** examining results. Avoid tuning a success threshold after seeing favourable trials.
3. Include negative cases and failures in denominators. Clearly distinguish operator-run QA, independently recruited user observations, verified paid commitments and buyer acquisition interest.
4. Protect participant identities and consent: no unsolicited recruitment, analytics integration, frame upload, external account connection or personal-data collection without separate owner/privacy approval.
5. Define a decision gate in advance: **continue, narrow use case, redesign, or stop**. Only claim competitive superiority when the same task has been measured against a relevant alternative on comparable devices under comparable conditions.
6. Preserve a provenance log with approved build SHA, test conditions, number of independent participants, number of attempts and reasons for exclusions. Keep individual records private and separate from published aggregate summaries.

**Real-world device:** unverified; licensing and HTTPS preview approval remain blockers.
